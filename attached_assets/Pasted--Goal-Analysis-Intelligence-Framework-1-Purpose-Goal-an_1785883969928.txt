# Goal Analysis Intelligence Framework

## 1. Purpose

Goal analysis is used to reconstruct the story of each goal:

* where the scoring possession began
* whether the opposition was organised
* how the ball progressed towards goal
* how the final opportunity was created
* how and where the goal was finished

A single goal may not accurately represent how a team normally plays. However, repeated patterns across multiple matches can provide useful evidence about:

* a team’s attacking identity
* a team’s defensive vulnerabilities
* the qualities of individual scorers and creators
* tactical considerations for an upcoming opponent

The application must distinguish between an isolated event and a repeatable pattern.

---

## 2. Goal Origin

Each goal is initially classified as either:

### Set Piece

* Corner
* Free kick
* Penalty

### Open Play Regain

* Front-third regain
* Middle-third regain
* Back-third regain

The regain location records where the scoring team most recently won possession before progressing to score.

This allows the analysis to work backwards from the goal and identify where the successful attacking sequence began.

---

## 3. Set-Piece Intelligence

Set-piece goals should be compared with relevant competition and historical benchmarks.

### Corner Goals

A team scoring above the expected benchmark from corners may demonstrate:

* strong delivery quality
* effective blocking or movement routines
* dominant aerial players
* good second-ball reactions
* deliberate set-piece preparation

An opponent conceding above the benchmark may demonstrate:

* weak marking
* poor first-contact success
* difficulty defending movement or screens
* poor goalkeeper control of the area
* weak organisation on second phases

### Free-Kick Goals

A strong free-kick scoring record may indicate:

* quality direct free-kick takers
* effective wide free-kick delivery
* rehearsed routines
* strong aerial or second-ball threats

Frequent free-kick concessions may indicate:

* poor defensive organisation
* difficulty defending deliveries
* unnecessary fouls in dangerous areas

### Penalty Goals

Penalty totals require careful interpretation.

A high number may indicate that the team:

* regularly enters the penalty area
* creates situations that force defenders into challenges
* has direct or skilful attackers
* maintains attacking pressure

However, penalties should not automatically be interpreted as evidence of superior finishing or open-play chance creation.

---

## 4. Regain Location

### Front-Third Regain Goals

A high proportion of goals beginning with front-third regains may be consistent with:

* aggressive pressing
* effective counter-pressing
* strong anticipation of opposition build-up
* forcing errors close to goal
* rapid exploitation of turnovers

However, the scoring pattern may also be influenced by opponents who:

* frequently attempt to play out from the back
* take excessive risks in build-up
* lack technical quality under pressure

The application should therefore avoid automatically stating that front-third regain goals prove the scoring team is an excellent pressing team.

A better interpretation is:

> The team shows strong evidence of creating goals through high regains. This may reflect effective pressing, although opposition build-up behaviour should also be considered.

For the opponent, repeated concessions from front-third turnovers may suggest:

* vulnerability when playing out
* poor support around the ball
* weak decision-making under pressure
* insufficient protection behind the first build-up line

---

### Middle-Third Regain Goals

Middle-third regains are likely to account for a large proportion of goals across a competition and may offer limited meaning when viewed alone.

Their value increases when combined with:

* transition state
* number of passes
* speed of attack
* progression channel
* final action

Middle-third regain goals scored quickly during transition may suggest:

* strong counter-attacking behaviour
* effective forward running
* early recognition of space
* an ability to exploit an unsettled defence

Middle-third regain goals scored after the opposition has reorganised may suggest:

* patience in possession
* an ability to circulate and progress the ball
* effective movement against a defensive block
* the capacity to create openings against organised opponents

---

### Back-Third Regain Goals

Goals beginning with a back-third regain require the team to progress the ball over a significant distance.

Repeated back-third regain goals may demonstrate:

* effective build-up from deep
* composure in possession
* good connections between defensive, midfield and attacking lines
* the ability to progress through or around pressure
* effective long-range transition attacks

The meaning depends heavily on transition state and the number of passes involved.

A fast back-third goal during transition may indicate:

* direct counter-attacking ability
* pace in forward areas
* effective early forward passing
* strong running beyond the ball

A back-third goal scored after transition may provide stronger evidence of:

* controlled possession
* sustained progression
* positional organisation
* the ability to break an organised defensive structure

For the conceding opponent, allowing a team to score after beginning in its own back third may suggest:

* poor collective defensive organisation
* ineffective pressing
* excessive space between lines
* weak recovery running
* an inability to delay or disrupt progression

This conclusion should be stronger when the goal is scored after transition, because the defending team had an opportunity to become organised.

---

## 5. Transition State

Every open-play goal is classified as:

### During Transition — DT

The goal is scored before the defending team has become organised following the loss of possession.

Common characteristics may include:

* rapid forward progression
* a low number of passes
* forward or diagonal running
* defenders retreating towards their own goal
* numerical or positional instability
* space available between or behind defenders

DT goals may identify a team that is especially dangerous in transition.

For the opponent, repeated DT concessions may suggest:

* poor rest defence
* insufficient protection behind attacks
* slow reactions after losing possession
* ineffective counter-pressing
* poor recovery runs
* players becoming disconnected after turnovers

---

### After Transition — AT

The defending team has had time to recover and become reasonably organised before the goal is scored.

AT goals may provide evidence that the scoring team can:

* retain possession
* move an organised defence
* create overloads
* find or manufacture space
* progress through established defensive lines
* produce effective final-third combinations

For the conceding team, repeated AT goals may suggest:

* difficulty defending for sustained periods
* poor compactness
* weak coordination between units
* an inability to protect key spaces
* ineffective pressure on the ball
* difficulty responding to switches, rotations or combinations

The distinction between DT and AT is central to the intelligence framework because the same regain location can represent very different football behaviour.

---

## 6. Passing Sequence

The number of passes between the regain and the goal helps describe the nature of the attack.

### Low-Pass Goals

Goals completed within approximately two or three passes may suggest:

* direct attacking intent
* rapid transition play
* effective early forward passing
* strong running beyond the ball
* efficient exploitation of an unsettled opponent

The interpretation becomes stronger when combined with a DT classification.

### Longer Passing Sequences

Goals involving longer passing sequences may suggest:

* controlled possession
* patient build-up
* strong support around the ball
* the ability to move the opposition
* effective positional structure

The interpretation becomes stronger when combined with an AT classification.

Pass count should not be judged as inherently good or bad. Its value is in identifying how the team most commonly creates successful attacks.

---

## 7. Progression Channel

Each goal records whether the attacking sequence primarily progressed through the:

* left channel
* central channel
* right channel

Repeated channel patterns may reveal:

* a preferred attacking side
* influential players or combinations
* overloads created in particular areas
* a reliance on wide or central progression
* positional weaknesses in the opposition

The application should investigate relationships between progression channel and:

* assist type
* scorer location
* player combinations
* transition state
* regain location

For example, a high number of right-channel attacks ending in cut-backs may identify a repeatable attacking pattern rather than merely a general preference for the right side.

---

## 8. Final-Pass Type

The final action before the goal may be classified as:

* Cross
* Cut-back
* Through ball
* Other pass or action

### Crosses

A high proportion of goals from crosses may suggest:

* strong wide progression
* effective delivery
* good penalty-area occupation
* aerial strength
* well-timed far-post or central runs

For the opponent, repeated concessions from crosses may suggest:

* difficulty preventing delivery
* poor box marking
* weak aerial defending
* poor tracking at the far post
* insufficient pressure on wide players

### Cut-Backs

Repeated cut-back goals may suggest:

* the ability to reach the byline
* effective movement behind the defensive line
* strong penalty-area timing
* players arriving into high-value central spaces
* an attacking style focused on creating close-range chances

For the opponent, repeated cut-back concessions may indicate:

* fullbacks or wide defenders being beaten
* defenders collapsing too close to goal
* poor tracking of late runners
* weak protection of central finishing areas

### Through Balls

Repeated through-ball goals may suggest:

* strong movement behind the defence
* players capable of recognising and executing penetrative passes
* an opposition defensive line that can be exploited
* direct central or half-space penetration

For the opponent, repeated concessions may suggest:

* poor pressure on the passer
* an excessively high or poorly coordinated defensive line
* centre-backs failing to cover depth
* midfielders failing to screen passing lanes

---

## 9. Finishing Method

Each goal records whether the scorer finished with:

* right foot
* left foot
* head
* another body part where relevant

It also records whether the finish was:

* first touch
* after taking one or more touches

### First-Touch Finishing

A high first-touch finishing rate may suggest:

* strong anticipation
* intelligent movement before receiving
* efficient penalty-area positioning
* composure under time pressure
* an ability to finish crosses, cut-backs or quick combinations

### Finishing After a Touch

Goals scored after controlling the ball may indicate:

* composure in one-on-one situations
* the ability to create a shooting angle
* strong ball manipulation
* time and space being available inside the scoring area

### Footedness and Heading

The finishing method helps build a scorer profile.

It may identify:

* dominant-foot reliance
* genuine two-footed ability
* aerial threat
* preferred service type
* possible defensive strategies against the player

The application should avoid labelling a player from a very small number of goals.

---

## 10. Scoring Location

Each goal includes an X and Y location so that goals can be plotted spatially.

This enables the application to identify:

* scoring hotspots
* preferred finishing zones
* central versus wide finishing patterns
* close-range versus longer-range tendencies
* player-specific scoring areas
* team-specific chance-creation locations

For a scorer, repeated locations may help build a finishing DNA profile.

Examples include:

* central penalty-area poacher
* far-post finisher
* edge-of-box shooter
* left-channel finisher
* six-yard-box specialist

For a defending team, concession hotspots may identify areas that are not being protected effectively.

---

## 11. Scorer and Assist Relationships

Recording the scorer and assisting player allows the system to identify repeated partnerships.

A recurring scorer–assist combination may indicate:

* strong player chemistry
* repeatable movement and passing relationships
* a dominant attacking partnership
* a particular tactical pattern
* dependence on specific players

This information may support opposition planning by identifying:

* the primary creator
* the preferred runner
* the most frequent service type
* whether disrupting one relationship may reduce the opponent’s threat

The system should also determine whether production is broadly distributed or concentrated among a small number of players.

---

## 12. Team Attacking Identity

Across a sufficient sample, combinations of goal metrics may provide evidence of team identity.

Possible identities include:

* high-regain pressing team
* fast-transition team
* possession and build-up team
* direct counter-attacking team
* crossing-focused team
* cut-back-focused team
* central combination team
* set-piece-dependent team
* team capable of breaking organised defensive blocks

Identity should be inferred from multiple related metrics rather than one isolated number.

Example:

**Front-third regains + DT goals + low pass count**

May indicate a high-pressing team that attacks quickly after winning possession.

**Back-third regains + AT goals + longer pass sequences**

May indicate a team capable of controlled build-up and progressing through organised opposition.

**Wide-channel progression + crosses + headed finishes**

May indicate a crossing-oriented attack with a strong aerial target.

---

## 13. Opponent Defensive Identity

The same goal data should be analysed from the conceding team’s perspective.

Possible vulnerabilities include:

* weak build-up under pressure
* poor rest defence
* slow defensive transition
* inability to defend organised attacks
* vulnerability to wide progression
* difficulty defending crosses
* poor protection against cut-backs
* weakness behind a high defensive line
* set-piece vulnerability
* dependence on individual defending rather than collective organisation

The app should explain whether a pattern is primarily:

* an attacking strength of the scoring team
* a defensive weakness of the opponent
* or potentially a combination of both

---

## 14. Evidence and Confidence

All interpretations should account for:

* number of goals
* number of matches
* percentage of the team’s total goals
* league benchmark
* historical benchmark
* consistency across opponents
* consistency across different periods of the season

Suggested confidence language:

* **Observation:** The pattern has occurred, but evidence is limited.
* **Emerging pattern:** The behaviour has repeated enough to warrant attention.
* **Strong evidence:** The pattern is clearly above the benchmark and consistent.
* **Established identity:** The pattern has persisted across a substantial sample.

The application should prefer wording such as:

> This pattern is consistent with…

> The available evidence suggests…

> This may indicate…

It should avoid definitive claims unless the evidence is sufficiently strong.

---

## 15. Insight Construction Template

Every automated goal-analysis insight should contain:

### Evidence

What occurred and how frequently?

### Comparison

How does it compare with the league, historical or team benchmark?

### Football Meaning

What football behaviours could explain the pattern?

### Alternative Explanation

Could opponent behaviour, match context or sample size also explain it?

### Confidence

How strong is the evidence?

### Coaching Implication

What might a coach investigate, prepare for or change?

### Video Review Question

What should the analyst check on footage before accepting the conclusion?

---

## 16. Core Product Principle

The goal-analysis engine must not simply report where goals came from.

It must explain:

> how the goal began, how the attack developed, why the opportunity may have emerged, what it suggests about both teams, and what a coach should investigate next.
